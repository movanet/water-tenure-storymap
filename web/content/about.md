# About this site

This site combines an interactive map with stories to show **who uses which water, and on what terms**, in one river basin. It is built with the open-source Water Tenure Storymap software.

**The content you are looking at is a synthetic example, not real data.** The Sample River Basin, its villages, its tenure records, its sources and its pictures are invented. They sit in the open sea near 0 degrees latitude and 0 degrees longitude so that nobody mistakes them for a real place. Replace this text by editing `web/content/about.md`.

## What a tenure record is

A tenure record describes one arrangement through which a group of users gains, holds or loses access to water: who the users are, what they use the water for, where and in which season, how access and allocation work, and how secure the arrangement is in law and in the holders' own view. Each record is classified on the FAO water tenure typology and cites its sources by code.

The typology distinguishes arrangements grounded in formal law (land-based rights, permits, licences, agency control, contracts, commonhold, de minimis use, reserves) from arrangements outside formal law (customary, religious-law, informal, assumed, impossible and unrecognised tenure). The Typology page lists every category and the ratings used by this deployment.

## Data protection

- No record names a private person or quotes a respondent.
- Records that could expose their users to harm are marked *aggregate only*. The map shows them at a displaced position, photos linked to them are displaced too, and the public exports leave them out.
- Photo uploads are stripped of camera metadata before they are stored.

## Software

This site runs [Water Tenure Storymap](https://github.com/movanet/water-tenure-storymap), open-source software (MIT licence) for publishing a water tenure assessment as a map and a story. The catalogue behind the map can be produced with the companion [water tenure skills](https://github.com/movanet/water-tenure-skills).
